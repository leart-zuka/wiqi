# Quantum Communication

Quantum communication is needed for the potential of a quantum internet. Currently, the internet is based on optical networks, with laser pulses and optical fibers. This allows communication between different locations, limited by the speed of light.
![Internet](/posts/assets/entries/qcommunication/Internet.jpg)

One of the key attractions of quantum mechanics, is the ability for particles to be entangled. This means that doing something on one particle can impact a second particle that can be very far away from it. While this seems like it would be instantaneous, the rules of quantum mechanics limit this. Check out more about quantum teleportation.
However, life is not quite as simple as this. Entanglement is very fragile, like a spider web and hard to achieve, especially over long distances.
![Web](/posts/assets/entries/qcommunication/Web.jpg)


Entanglement can easily be broken, and we cannot simply create entanglement over large distances. However, it is possible to form strong entanglement, or connections over short distances. Like a spiderweb, we can strengthen this at certain points, to allow for longer distances. We use quantum repeaters, which help to overcome the distance limitations and losses that are experienced in real networks.
