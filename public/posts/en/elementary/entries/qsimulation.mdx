# Quantum Simulation

Quantum systems in general behave differently than classical (or "normal") systems. In order to understand physical systems, researchers often make use of simulations, which mimic physical processes in a computer program. Simulations can often save time and resources compared to real experiments. However, since many important areas of research and applications in industry, like e.g. chemical reactions, material properties or magnetism, are governed by quantum effects and are therefore quantum systems, we need to introduce quantum simulations. As quantum systems exhibit different properties compared to classical systems, a classical computer is not able to simulate many large quantum systems in a reasonable amount of time, because the necessary calculations would just take too long. To address this problem, researchers make use of so-called quantum simulators, which are quantum systems, that carry out a physical process in a controllable way. One could say, that the quantum nature of the simulator is used to speed up the simulation of a quantum system. This realizes a so-called quantum simulation. With such a quantum simulation, e.g. the interactions of electrons during chemical reactions, or their behavior in certain materials can be studied and understood.
