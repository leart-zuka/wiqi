## What is an ion?
Atoms usually have an equal number of protons and electrons. An ion is formed when an atom loses or gains an electron, then, we say it has an electrical charge. We are interested in ions because they are very stable and they can hold information for a long time. That means that they would make a very good platform for a quantum computer, where ions act as qubits.

## Why do we need to trap them?
To build a quantum computer with ions, we need them to be still and stable. An ion trap keeps them hovering in one place so we can control them with extreme precision. For example, we can shine light on them (a laser) which will change their state. With clever engineering, a combination of light acting on the atoms will perform calculations and  run algorithms, making the whole system a quantum computer. For this, the trap is very important, because if ions were flying around freely, we would never be able to aim the lasers accurately enough to work with them!

## How do we trap ions?
First of all we need a vacuum, which is an environment where most of the air has been removed. If an ion bumps into a molecule of air or a speck of dust, it could be knocked out of the trap, and our calculation would be ruined.
Before actually trapping the ions, they need to be moving very slowly. To make them slower, we need to make them colder, because that means they have less energy to move. We actually use specialized lasers to hit the ions and make them lose energy (this is called laser cooling).
Now to the interesting question: how do we actually trap them? Remember ions have an electrical charge, so we cannot trap them using a constant electric field, because the ion would experience a force only in one direction. The Paul trap solves this by using electric fields that switch back and forth incredibly fast. These fields make the ion move back and forth quickly, so overall it appears to stay in the same place. It is like keeping a marble in the center of a bowl that is constantly shaking: if the motion is fast enough, the marble can still remain in the middle. This idea is used in the so-called Paul trap.
